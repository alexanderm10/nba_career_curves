The shape of a shooting career

A player's true shooting percentage in any one season mixes three things: who he is, how he is going right now, and how the bounces went. A latent-talent model pulls those apart for 1,007 players and 6,274 seasons. It shows a career curve the league average hides, and it forecasts the next season better than any simple rule we tested.

What did not add value

A player's style last season (big or perimeter, creator or spot-up) did not improve the forecast: 3.37 against 3.36. The style effects are real, but the permanent talent term already carries them. Prior minutes were also no better than years. Strong and weak teams do sit in different parts of style space, but that gap is tangled with roster spread and team quality, so it supports no claim that a style wins games.

Limits

Box scores are a coarse view of shooting; tracking and shot-location data would sharpen every piece of this. Long careers are a selected group, which the within-player model handles better than the averages but not perfectly. The curve is quadratic in years. A flexible version peaks earlier, around year four, and shows the same late decline. Style scores enter the model as if known exactly.

Data and method. ESPN box scores via hoopR, 2002 to 2026 regular seasons, All-Star games removed. Talent model: player level + AR(1) form across seasons + known sampling error, fit in Stan (CmdStan 2.40, 4 chains, Rhat ≤ 1.01, no divergences). Every model was scored against simple rivals on the same held-out seasons.
